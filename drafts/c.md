OPEN INTEREST / Daily brief, format v2. Sunday, 22 November 2026.

LEAD: Chainflip says its vault theft also took 122.86 bitcoin.

The cross-chain exchange disclosed the Bitcoin loss in a notice offering the person who took the funds a 10% bounty. Chainflip Labs wants the rest back by 18:38 UTC on 23 November 2026. [https://chainflip.io/return]

At 07:10 UTC on 21 November 2026, a single Ethereum transaction moved 520.22 ether, about 3.25 million USDC and 758,141 USDT out of Chainflip’s vault. [https://eth.blockscout.com/tx/0xc928950f79b4e4697af674310d1f3a08c2659adc4d60e719f3af90ab023ff20f] [https://docs.chainflip.io/protocol/supported-chains-assets/mainnet-addresses] Bitquery valued the Ethereum outflow at about $4.86 million. That estimate covers the ether and stablecoins, before adding the bitcoin Chainflip later listed. [https://bitquery.io/investigations/chainflip-hack] [https://chainflip.io/return]

Bitquery found that the Ethereum vault accepted the payout under a valid signature. Chainflip’s documentation describes a system in which validators jointly sign vault payments. [https://bitquery.io/investigations/chainflip-hack] [https://assets.chainflip.io/whitepaper.pdf] Chainflip Labs says it identified how the signing key was exposed and took steps to prevent a recurrence. Its public notice gives no technical account of the fix. [https://chainflip.io/return]

Chainflip’s swap page showed its services unavailable on 22 November 2026. [https://swap.chainflip.io/] Chainflip’s documentation says its most restrictive emergency setting lets the chain keep producing blocks while it stops swap payouts and liquidity withdrawals. [https://docs.chainflip.io/protocol/governance-and-security] After a separate theft of about 736,000 USDT from its Tron vault on 12 September 2026, validators approved using vault surplus to repay affected liquidity providers. [https://chainflip.io/blog/getting-chainflip-back-up-and-running] [https://chainflip.io/blog/proposal-008-passed-making-tron-usdt-lps-whole]

The offer asks for 110.58 bitcoin and 2,618.1 ether. Bitquery traced the stablecoin proceeds through swaps into ether, the currency Chainflip is asking to get back. [https://chainflip.io/return] [https://bitquery.io/investigations/chainflip-hack] Chainflip Labs promises to withdraw its own complaints if both payments arrive by the deadline. The notice sets no timetable for repaying users. [https://chainflip.io/return]

[TECH] Stellar puts quantum-resistant signature checks to a mainnet vote.
The Stellar Development Foundation lists the validator vote on the Bell upgrade for 17:00 UTC on 23 November 2026. [https://stellar.org/blog/developers/introducing-bell-protocol-30-on-stellar] Bell would let smart contracts check quantum-resistant ML-DSA signatures while classic accounts would still use their existing transaction signatures. [https://stellar.org/blog/developers/introducing-bell-protocol-30-on-stellar] It would also record ledger close times to the millisecond, a step the foundation says removes a barrier to faster closes. [https://stellar.org/blog/developers/introducing-bell-protocol-30-on-stellar]

[CULTURE] Uniswap voters back extending v4 protocol fees to five more chains.
The preliminary ballot closed on 21 November 2026 with 27.9 million votes in favour, clearing the 10 million quorum with no opposition. [https://snapshot.org/#/s:uniswapgovernance.eth/proposal/0x28780882407a7a45326f0d72a036c02cb4a48591813d7847afd87e30e8e17f8c] Uniswap Labs wants v4 fees on Celo, Soneium, X Layer, World Chain and Zora, where it says older versions already collect protocol fees. [https://gov.uniswap.org/t/temp-check-protocol-fee-expansion-v4-on-five-more-chains/26349] The proposal still needs an on-chain vote after a companion vote to upgrade the fee software. [https://gov.uniswap.org/t/temp-check-protocol-fee-expansion-v4-on-five-more-chains/26349] Uniswap’s fee programme grew out of a proposal by Hayden Adams, Ken Ng and Devin Walsh to use protocol revenue to retire UNI tokens. [https://blog.uniswap.org/unification]
